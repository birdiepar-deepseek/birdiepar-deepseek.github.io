A surprise-risk score is not a win probability. A score of 55 does not mean a candidate has a 55% chance of winning. It is a 0-100 forecast-fragility score that highlights where conventional expectations may be more vulnerable to structural change or missing data.

Baseline Forecast Layer

The baseline forecast layer summarizes conventional race information such as race ratings, incumbency, polling, fundraising context, prior results, and district or state partisanship. It is the dashboard's starting point, not the main contribution.

Turnout Composition Shock Index

The Turnout Composition Shock Index looks for evidence that the actual electorate may differ from the historically expected electorate. It uses proxy signals such as registered-voter versus likely-voter polling gaps, sample-mix notes, turnout warnings, and other structured evidence when those signals are available.

Cohort Activation Index

The Cohort Activation Index tracks age- or generation-specific activation only when race-specific evidence exists. If the public record does not contain race-level cohort evidence, the index stays neutral rather than inventing movement.

Trump Endorsement Shock Index

The Trump Endorsement Shock Index separates primary consolidation effects from possible general-election backlash effects. It does not assume that an endorsement is always helpful or always harmful; it records whether the endorsement changes the race's structural fragility.

Candidate / Local Shock Index

The Candidate / Local Shock Index tracks candidate-quality, recruitment, scandal or legal-risk, open-seat, and local issue dynamics when source-backed evidence exists. It is meant to capture race-specific conditions that broad national models may smooth over.

Overall Surprise Risk Score

The Overall Surprise Risk Score is a forecast-fragility score that combines structural signal and missing-data uncertainty. It is not a probability, and higher values mean the race may be harder for conventional expectations to interpret confidently.

Missing Data

The dashboard intentionally shows missing data rather than hiding it. Sparse polling, unresolved candidate fields, or limited cohort evidence can make a race uncertain without proving movement toward either party.

Data Classes

Observed data are direct public inputs such as ratings or reported figures. Proxy signals are indirect signs that may illuminate turnout, candidate, or coordination risk. Analyst assumptions are explicit judgment calls used to bridge gaps. Model outputs are computed scores and labels created from those inputs.

Limitations

This public site omits detailed internal reports, source logs, raw working inputs, and analyst-assumption files. The public version is designed to show topline outputs, charts, methodology, and limitations without exposing private working files.
